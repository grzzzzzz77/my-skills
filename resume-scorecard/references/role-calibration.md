## 1. Select A Profile

Choose one `role_family` for each resume:

- `engineering_data`
- `product_design`
- `sales_growth_ops`
- `corporate_functions`
- `research_professional`
- `leadership_management`
- `general`

Use `general` when the target is unclear or crosses several families. Keep the common axis weights unchanged so score vectors remain comparable; change the accepted evidence and anchor interpretation, not the arithmetic.

## 2. Shared Calibration Rules

- Evaluate actual scope, decisions, artifacts, and impact; never award points for employer prestige alone.
- Treat school, employer, certification, award, or publication reputation as external validation only when relevant and accurately described.
- Do not require revenue metrics from roles that primarily create quality, safety, research, policy, design, enablement, or risk value.
- Compare the candidate with the selected stage's expected ownership, not with the most senior people in the role family.
- For career changers, value transferable evidence and learning velocity; keep missing target-specific history in JD fit or relevance, not as a blanket penalty across all axes.

## 3. Role-Family Profiles

### `engineering_data`

Strong evidence includes:

- production systems, data pipelines, models, platforms, applications, infrastructure, or security controls
- technical constraints, tradeoffs, failure modes, scale, reliability, performance, cost, or developer productivity
- design decisions and personal ownership of implementation, rollout, operation, or incident learning
- code, architecture, tests, observability, adoption, or delivery artifacts

Avoid:

- rewarding a long technology list without use cases
- requiring public business metrics for internal or infrastructure work
- treating feature count as equivalent to technical depth

### `product_design`

Strong evidence includes:

- user/problem discovery, prioritization, product or design decisions, experiments, prototypes, and shipped experiences
- research synthesis, information architecture, interaction systems, design systems, roadmaps, and cross-functional alignment
- adoption, usability, conversion, task success, support reduction, stakeholder acceptance, or documented decision quality
- portfolio artifacts and explicit contribution boundaries

Avoid:

- judging product/design work only by engineering keywords
- treating visual decoration as product impact
- requiring revenue numbers when user, workflow, or decision evidence is stronger

### `sales_growth_ops`

Strong evidence includes:

- revenue, pipeline, conversion, retention, activation, acquisition, customer tier, territory, budget, channel, or operating volume
- sales cycle, account complexity, experimentation, campaign mechanics, operational systems, and repeatable playbooks
- ranking, quota, renewals, collections, cost efficiency, cycle-time, service level, or process-risk outcomes
- individual contribution separated from team totals

Avoid:

- rewarding large team numbers without personal attribution
- comparing enterprise and transactional sales without cycle/context
- requiring technical depth unrelated to the target role

### `corporate_functions`

Strong evidence includes:

- finance, legal, HR, procurement, compliance, administration, audit, policy, and internal-control deliverables
- budgets, entities, jurisdictions, headcount, vendors, cases, controls, close cycles, hiring funnels, or stakeholder range
- risk avoided, accuracy, timeliness, compliance, savings, process reliability, or decision support
- certifications, licensed authority, and repeat responsibility when relevant

Avoid:

- marking confidential work as weak merely because exact figures are absent
- treating routine volume alone as high complexity
- importing software-engineering ownership standards

### `research_professional`

Strong evidence includes:

- research questions, methods, datasets, experiments, publications, patents, clinical/professional cases, teaching, standards, or domain deliverables
- novelty, methodological rigor, author/contributor role, venue context, replication, citation, adoption, or expert review
- ethical, safety, regulatory, or evidentiary constraints
- specialist credentials and sustained domain development

Avoid:

- equating publication count with contribution quality
- ignoring author order, role, venue, or methodological contribution
- forcing commercial impact when research or professional impact is the appropriate value type

### `leadership_management`

Strong evidence includes:

- team, portfolio, budget, program, business line, architecture, or organizational scope
- strategy, prioritization, operating mechanisms, hiring, coaching, succession, governance, and stakeholder decisions
- durable results, risk control, organizational capability, and repeatable systems beyond one-off heroics
- explicit distinction between personal decisions, team outcomes, and inherited context

Avoid:

- awarding leadership points for title alone
- attributing all team outcomes to one person
- overvaluing tactical detail at the expense of organizational scope

### `general`

Use universal evidence: relevance, complexity, ownership, value, depth, growth, clear positioning, and defensibility. Keep confidence at medium when the intended lane is unknown.

## 4. Career-Stage Overlays

Choose one `candidate_stage`:

### `intern_entry`

- Accept coursework, substantial projects, internships, competitions, research, volunteering, and early work as primary evidence.
- Look for learning velocity, real constraints, contribution clarity, and credible artifacts.
- Do not require organization-wide impact or independent strategy ownership.

### `early_career`

- Expect production-like delivery, independently owned tasks or modules, increasing complexity, and clear collaboration.
- Strong scores require several defensible outcomes or artifacts, not management scope.

### `experienced_ic`

- Expect independent problem ownership, meaningful tradeoffs, cross-team work, and repeatable delivery quality.
- Strong scores require evidence beyond task execution.

### `senior_ic`

- Expect system/domain-level decisions, ambiguous problem solving, durable impact, mentorship or technical influence, and risk management.
- Do not require people management when the target is an IC track.

### `manager_lead`

- Expect team/program scope, operating mechanisms, talent or stakeholder leadership, strategic prioritization, and durable organizational results.
- Separate inherited outcomes from interventions the candidate made.

### `career_changer`

- Evaluate prior-lane career capital fairly and surface transferable skills.
- Put target-specific gaps mainly in `relevance_trajectory` and `jd_fit`.
- Do not erase credible prior achievements because the target has changed.

## 5. Cross-Industry Use

For cross-industry comparison:

1. Score each resume with its own role family and stage overlay.
2. Compare axis scores with confidence and evidence coverage visible.
3. Compare normalized qualities: demonstrated capital, communication, transferability, risk, and upgrade effort.
4. Name scenario winners instead of a universal winner.
5. Do not compare JD keyword counts across unrelated roles.
